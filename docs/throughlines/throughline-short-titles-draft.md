## Overlaps worth resolving before building pages

**Vantage cluster — 8, 14, 24.** All three describe the artist's implied
position climbing from ground level to orbit. #8 is the full three-decade arc;
#14 says Self Portrait 1995 held both ends at once; #24 traces the same ascent
across the founding four Art Collision paintings. These may be one throughline
with three pieces of evidence rather than three claims. If they stay separate,
each needs to say how it differs from the others.

**Photograph/paint cluster — 20, 21, 23, 25, 26.** #20 is the foundational
claim (photograph settled, paint open). #23 refines it into two models
(Polke opposition, Hamilton continuity). #21 says the hybrid was unrepeated.
#25 says it returned in 2022. #26 reads the same arc as growing confidence.
This is the densest cluster in the archive and arguably the practice's central
argument — worth deciding whether it's one throughline with stages, or a parent
with children.

**Self-taught pair — 15, 16.** "Not knowing it was impossible" and "self-taught
where no school existed" are close enough that a reader would struggle to tell
them apart. #16 is explicitly broader (skating, HTML, Transition, Artism).

**Romanticism pair — 9, 19.** #9 is the structural homology with Friedrich's
audience; #19 is the invented-to-lived arc via the Peace Walk. #19 largely
contains #9.

## A category question

Several entries are claims about **media history** rather than about the
practice: **29** (moral credit across three wars), **30** (why Korea is
absent), **33** (one name, three wars), and arguably **31** (anachronism).
These are research findings underpinning Mediums of War — true, interesting,
and worth keeping — but they describe the twentieth century, not a recurrence
across Bolter's work.

Two others — **4** (no time left to lie) and **5** (speaker, scribe, archive) —
are statements about the archive project itself rather than about the paintings.

Worth deciding whether throughlines are strictly *recurrences across works*, or
whether they also hold *positions and findings*. If strictly the former, these
six want a different home — series notes, or a separate "positions" collection.
Either answer is defensible; mixing them without saying so makes the set harder
to read.
